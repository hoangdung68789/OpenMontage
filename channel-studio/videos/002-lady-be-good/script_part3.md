### S5.1 · 12:52–13:55 · Đi về hướng biển

They had no way of knowing that no one would come for sixteen years.

### S5.2 · 13:55–15:02 · Vì sao lại là hướng bắc
> Why north-west? [pause] Because, as far as the crew knew, the coast was close. They believed they had just come in from the Mediterranean, and that the sea, and the British and American bases along it, were only a short walk behind them. Walk north, and sooner or later you reach the shore. [pause] It was the same mistake as the night before, now made on foot. The bomber had not just crossed the coast. It had flown on for hours, deep into Libya. The wreck would later be found some four hundred and forty miles south-east of Benghazi. [pause] So every mile the men walked was a mile in the right direction, toward a coast that was simply much too far away. Toner kept writing as they went. One short entry says only: still going.

### S5.4 · 15:49–16:48 · Ba người đi tiếp

### S5.6 · 17:32–18:33 · Cuốn sổ
> It is worth pausing on what that diary is. [pause] It is not a memoir, written years later by a survivor. No one in this story survived. It is a handful of short, dated lines, written in pencil-thin moments of strength, by a co-pilot who did not know how his story would end. Eight days in the desert, in a few pages. [pause] Every quote you have heard in this chapter comes from it, as published by the U.S. National Archives and by Air and Space Forces Magazine. We have not added words to it. [pause] And we only know any of this because, seventeen years later, someone found the diary lying beside the men who wrote in it, and carried it home.

### S5.7 · 18:33–19:21 · Ba người trong biển cát
